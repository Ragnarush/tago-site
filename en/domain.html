Your domain name

A domain name of your own, like mybusiness.ca, gives you a business address like info@mybusiness.ca. With Brevo, your invoices go out from that address and stay out of spam. This page shows buying the domain from Cloudflare, connecting it to Brevo and getting replies in your Gmail, as they were on October 25, 2026. If a label has moved, look for the closest word.

The domain is not only for email: it is also the address of your future website. The day you build one, you connect it to this same domain, with nothing more to buy.

1. Already have a domain?

If your business already has a website (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy or other), it already has a domain: use that one, nothing to buy. Go straight to authenticating it with Brevo: the records go wherever the domain's DNS is managed, often at the website host. Brevo's Automatic method does it for you if it recognizes that provider.

If another service already receives your email on that domain (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, the host), do not touch the existing MX or SPF records. Brevo needs neither: it only adds its code and its two DKIM records. And a domain has only one DMARC: if one already exists, keep it.

2. Buying the domain from Cloudflare

Any registrar will do, Canadian or not. This guide shows Cloudflare because it sells domains at cost, with no markup, renewals included, and offers free DNS and email forwarding (section 4). Cloudflare is an American company: it knows your registrant details, but removes them from the public WHOIS directory.

Create a free account at dash.cloudflare.com. In the left menu: Domains › Registrations, then Buy domain. Search for the name you want. A .ca cost US$9.19 a year on October 25, 2026; prices are in US dollars.

Pick a short name that is easy to spell out over the phone, without hyphens if you can. A .ca tells your clients right away that you are in Canada.

The form

Tap the domain you chose. The form asks for your registrant details. Leave Automatic renewal on: an expired domain is an email address that stops receiving, and a name someone else can buy.

A .ca requires a Canadian address and a connection to Canada (the Canadian Presence Requirements). As a self-employed person, pick your personal situation under Registrant type, for example Canadian citizen or Permanent Resident of Canada. The Organization field is only for an incorporated business or another organization.

Then tap Continue to pay. A domain is non-refundable: check the spelling before you pay. After the purchase, a third party handling the .ca may email you to verify your address: follow it, or the domain may be suspended.

3. Connecting the domain to Brevo

In Brevo, add the domain as shown in the Brevo walkthrough, then pick the method:

  • Automatic: if Brevo recognizes Cloudflare, it opens a Cloudflare window, you sign in and allow the change. Brevo creates the records itself.
  • Manual: you copy each record into Cloudflare. In Cloudflare, open your domain, then DNS › Records and Add record.

In manual mode, for each record Brevo shows: pick the Type (TXT or CNAME), paste the Name and the value (Content for a TXT, Target for a CNAME), then Save. Cloudflare appends your domain to the name on its own: type brevo1._domainkey, not brevo1._domainkey.mybusiness.ca.

For both CNAME records (DKIM 1 and DKIM 2), Proxy status must be DNS only, the grey cloud. Cloudflare often sets it on its own for these names, but check: an orange cloud ("Proxied") keeps Brevo from validating the DKIM.

Then go back to Brevo: Verify records, then Authenticate domain. At Cloudflare, a change usually spreads within a few minutes.

4. Getting the replies in your Gmail

Buying a domain does not create a mailbox: with nothing more, a client who replies to info@mybusiness.ca gets an error. Email Routing, free at Cloudflare, forwards what arrives at that address to your Gmail or Outlook.

In the left menu: Compute › Email Service › Email Routing, then Onboard Domain.

  1. Pick your domain. Cloudflare shows the records it will add (MX, SPF and DKIM): accept.
  2. Create a custom address, for example info, that sends to an email address, with your Gmail address as the destination.
  3. Cloudflare sends a verification email to your Gmail: open it and confirm. Until you do, nothing is forwarded.
  4. Test it: write to info@mybusiness.ca from another address.

Email Routing and Brevo don't get in each other's way: one receives (MX), the other sends (Brevo code and DKIM). You then reply from Gmail; your client will see your Gmail address, unless you add info@ as a "send mail as" address in Gmail.

5. In TAGO

In your business profile, set info@mybusiness.ca as the email: it is the address printed on your invoices and the one Brevo uses as the sender. The rest is set up as in the Brevo walkthrough.

6. If it doesn't work

Brevo can't find the records

Wait a few minutes, then check again. Next, compare each name in Cloudflare: the name must not contain the domain twice (brevo1._domainkey.mybusiness.ca.mybusiness.ca). Finally, both CNAME records must be DNS only, grey cloud.

"Only one DMARC": there already is one

If DNS › Records already shows a TXT named _dmarc, don't create Brevo's: two DMARC records cancel each other out. Keep the existing one.

Email to info@ doesn't reach Gmail

Check that the destination address is confirmed (Cloudflare's verification email, section 4) and look in Gmail's spam folder.
